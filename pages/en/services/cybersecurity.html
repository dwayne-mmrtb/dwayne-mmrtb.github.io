Cybersecurity for small businesses

Small businesses are now the main target of ransomware precisely because they're less protected. The good news: a handful of well-applied measures stops the vast majority of attacks.

The measures that actually matter

  • Multi-factor authentication (MFA) on email, remote access and every admin account.
  • Immutable, off-site backups that an attacker can't encrypt or delete, with restore tests.
  • Up-to-date patching on workstations, servers, firewalls and VPNs.
  • Least privilege: nobody works day to day with an admin account.
  • Endpoint protection: next-generation antivirus (EDR) and disk encryption.
  • Microsoft 365 security: conditional access, phishing protection and suspicious sign-in alerts.
  • Staff awareness of phishing emails and payment fraud.

Our approach

We start by assessing where you stand on these measures, then give you a priority list ranked by risk and cost. You decide what gets done and when; we handle the implementation.

Québec's Law 25

Since Law 25 came into force in Québec, businesses must protect the personal information they hold and report confidentiality incidents. The technical measures above are a big part of meeting that obligation.

Frequently asked questions

We have antivirus. Isn't that enough?

No. Antivirus is one layer among several. Most successful attacks come through a stolen password without MFA or an unpatched flaw, not through a detectable virus.

What should we do if we're attacked?

Unplug affected computers from the network without shutting them down, and contact us right away. Don't pay a ransom before your backups have been assessed.
